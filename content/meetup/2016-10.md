## Talks

### Barrier Pattern in Go (with Context)

Gerrit Renker examined the barrier pattern as a way to fan out work to multiple goroutines and wait for all of them to finish. He began with `context.Context` — its `Done`, `Err`, `Deadline`, and `Value` methods, the convention of passing it as the first argument, and how `WithCancel`/`WithDeadline`/`WithTimeout` form nested trees that can cancel whole groups of goroutines. From there he introduced the barrier itself: `sync.WaitGroup`, the Go equivalent of the classic fork-join pattern, along with its pitfalls (such as a goroutine panicking before calling `Done`). The second half covered `golang.org/x/sync/errgroup`, which extends `WaitGroup` with error capture and cancellation — its `Go` and `Wait` methods, how `Wait` returns the first error and triggers cancel, and why that matters for cancelling sibling work when one task fails. He closed with a worked example spawning stub VMs in parallel.

Key points:
- `context.Context` carries cancellation, deadlines, and values down a call tree.
- Pass `ctx` as the first argument; derive children with `WithCancel`/`WithTimeout`.
- `sync.WaitGroup` implements the fork-join barrier pattern.
- A panic before `wg.Done()` can leave the barrier stuck.
- `errgroup.Group` adds error capture and cancellation to `WaitGroup`.
- On first error, errgroup cancels the shared context to stop sibling work.
- Useful for fan-out tasks like provisioning resources in parallel.
