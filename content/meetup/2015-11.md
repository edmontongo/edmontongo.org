## Talks

### Messaging patterns and job queues in Go

Gerrit Renker gave a tour of message-oriented middleware (MOM) and job queues, and how Go fits in. He contrasted asynchronous, decoupled messaging ("mail") with synchronous RPC ("telephone"), covering queue and pub/sub endpoint models, brokers, and delivery guarantees (at-most-once, at-least-once, exactly-once). The talk traced enterprise MOM history from the Information Bus through IBM MQ, MSMQ, and JMS to AMQP and RabbitMQ, then surveyed the Go ecosystem: `streadway/amqp` and `rabbit-hole` for RabbitMQ, ZeroMQ and nanomsg for high-speed messaging, Shopify's `sarama` for Kafka, plus NATS, NSQ, Redis (`rmq`), Gearman, beanstalkd, `machinery`, and STOMP. It closed with pattern libraries — `go-kit` (rate limiter, circuit breaker, load balancer, tracing) and `go-resiliency` (circuit breaker, deadline/timeout, batching, retrier) — and the observation that while MOM breadth is huge, Go's strengths lie in the depth of its concurrency.

Key points:
- MOM decouples senders and receivers; RPC couples them.
- Endpoint models: point-to-point vs publish/subscribe.
- QoS: at-most-once, at-least-once (needs idempotence), exactly-once.
- Enterprise lineage: TIB → IBM MQ/MSMQ → JMS → AMQP/RabbitMQ.
- Go clients and platforms: `streadway/amqp`, `rabbit-hole`, `sarama` (Kafka), NATS, NSQ.
- ZeroMQ/nanomsg trade reliability for performance; job queues include Redis `rmq`, resque/goworker, Gearman, beanstalkd, `machinery`.
- Resilience patterns live in `go-kit` and `go-resiliency`.
