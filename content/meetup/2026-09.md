### Unbounded Concurrency

Peter Preeper gave a guided tour of concurrency in Go by evolving a TCP port scanner through ten stages — from a naive sequential version to a bounded, cancellable, fan-out/fan-in pipeline. Along the way he demonstrated the classic `go` trap (goroutines that outlive the program), synchronizing with `sync.WaitGroup` (including Go 1.25's `WaitGroup.Go`), why unbounded concurrency blows up, and how to bound it with a worker pool and a semaphore. Later versions added timeouts, composed small stages into a pipeline, and applied fan-out/fan-in with graceful shutdown.

Key points:
- Start simple; only add concurrency when it pays off.
- Always know when your goroutines are done.
- Bound your concurrency — unbounded goroutines exhaust resources.
- Use directional channels to express intent in APIs.
- Compose pipelines from small stages; use fan-out/fan-in for throughput.
- Handle timeouts/cancellation and shut down gracefully.
